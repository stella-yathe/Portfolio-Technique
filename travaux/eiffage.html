1 · The business

Two businesses in one group

Eiffage reported €25.3bn of revenue in 2025 (excluding IFRIC 12). Concessions, mainly the APRR motorway network, are 16% of revenue but about 64% of current operating income before holding costs. Works (construction, infrastructure, energy systems) are 84% of revenue. A single DCF would not do justice to two businesses with such different risk, which is why the valuation is done by parts.

Net debt fell from €10.5bn to €9.9bn while EBITDA rose from €3.2bn to €4.4bn: the group deleveraged through performance. Net income grew more slowly (+6.4% a year) mainly because of the jump in the effective tax rate. The ratios are computed from the financial statements rather than from the aggregates published by the company.

3 · Valuation method

One DCF per block, each with its own WACC

Works: five years of explicit free cash flow to the firm (2026-2030), then a Gordon-Shapiro terminal value with growth of 0.9%. The terminal value is 69% of the €8.0bn enterprise value, which makes this block sensitive to margin and WACC.
Eiffarie (APRR, AREA, Aliaé): cash flows until the concessions return to the State in 2035-2036 without indemnity, so no terminal value.
Other concessions: explicit flows to 2040, then an annuity to the average maturity of 2060.

5 · Sensitivity

The value holds between €157 and €207

For a WACC shift of ±0.9 pt and Works terminal growth between 0.3% and 1.5%. One point of WACC moves the value by about €18 per share, one point of growth by about €9. Taking the Works EBITDA margin from 6.5% to 6.0% costs €14 per share.

6 · Interpretation

What would explain the gap with the market?

Even with prudent assumptions the model stays above the market price. An overall discount of 44% would be needed to bring the central value down to the share price, and 33% from the prudent case. Read the other way, the share price roughly values Works alone: keeping the model values for the concessions and Getlink (€83 per share) leaves about €15 for Works, around 0.3x EBITDA against 4.6x in the model. Neither reading is plausible on its own. The market probably applies discounts across several blocks at once.

What supports the value

  • Healthy fundamentals: 8% annual growth, stable EBITDA margin, 2.3x net debt / EBITDA
  • Motorway concessions with a 70% EBITDA margin and highly visible cash flows
  • Converging methods: SOTP, multiples and analyst range

What could justify a discount

  • APRR network returned to the State in 2035-2036 without indemnity
  • Tax and tariff risk on motorways
  • Holding discount on a two-business group; net income down on a 36.6% tax rate; weak new-build market

Limits of the model

  • Other concessions at 12.8x EBITDA: margin and capex copied from APRR
  • Credit spreads and ownership percentages are not observable
  • Five peers only; Getlink share price and analyst consensus need updating
